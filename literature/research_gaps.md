# Evidence-based research gap analysis (provisional)

| Existing research evidence | Identified limitation | Research gap | Candidate direction / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dedeene et al. (2024) reports large held-out time windows at one regional lab network; several recent studies use single-center cohorts. | Evidence for transport to independent health systems, analyzer platforms, and populations is limited; paper performance is not external validation outside the originating network. | Need transparent cross-source validation with explicit target/feature harmonization and population-shift reporting. | Train on one source and evaluate untouched on another only if labels/feature definitions can be harmonized; otherwise run separate tasks and state the comparison is non-equivalent. Report calibration drift and subgroup sensitivity. |
| Farashi & Momtaz (2025) reused the Taylor ED dataset for feature selection and model comparisons; Taylor is one ED-system cohort. | Reuse of the same cohort is not independent generalization; feature selection and repeated CV must be isolated from the final test split. | Need leakage-resistant reproduction with nested or train-only selection, patient grouping, and external assessment. | Reconstruct the documented 18-feature ensemble while doing all data-driven selection/tuning on training folds; stratify/group by patient IDs if available; preserve a sealed test set. |
| Wasfy et al. (2026) reports 2,530 sample records, 2,294 unique IDs among 2,529 identified records, sample-level stratified random split, AUROC 0.858 but sensitivity 0.587. | Repeated patient records may cross folds; sensitivity is inadequate for rule-out despite apparently strong discrimination; no centre/date identifiers prevent group/time validation. | Need assess patient-level splitting and clinically prespecified operating thresholds; AUROC alone does not demonstrate safe rule-out. | Use patient-group holdout where identifiers exist and report sensitivity, specificity, NPV/PPV, false negatives, PR-AUC, and confidence intervals at a validation-only threshold. |
| Sergounioti et al. (2025) reports threshold optimization and different sensitivity/specificity operating points; dataset excludes symptom context and is not open. | Performance depends on intended use and threshold; clinical symptoms/contexts can be missing, and complete-case selection may bias results. | Need evaluate both feature availability settings, sensitivity-first screening vs specificity-first culture triage, and missing-data robustness. | Predefine a validation threshold (e.g., >=80% specificity as a screening operating point; also report sensitivity at >=90% specificity), compare UA-only with broader clinical data only when available. Do not use the final test set to select thresholds. |
| Lin et al. (2026) uses SHAP on a small pediatric cohort and reports internal random validation only. | Small validation positive count, single institution and pediatric-specific sample; global feature importance does not establish causality or transportability. | Need calibrated, subgroup-aware explanations in independent populations and assay domains. | Explain only after external evaluation; assess stability of SHAP/permutation rankings across resamples and document feature/assay mapping. Avoid presenting explanations as causal. |
| Studies target both positive urine culture and diagnosed UTI / suspected UTI; Taylor ED cohort is symptomatic-context selected, ARMD culture records include microbiological culture positivity. | Labels differ in thresholds and clinical context; “positive culture” is not interchangeable with symptomatic infection. | Need outcome-specific tasks and an explicit label harmonization decision before cross-dataset comparisons. | Use separate endpoints (e.g., source culture positivity vs source paper UTI proxy); do not pool, recalibrate jointly, or claim external validation if the definition is not comparable. |
| Model reports commonly emphasize AUROC or accuracy, while class imbalance and rule-out implications vary. | Limited reporting of calibration, uncertainty, confidence intervals, decision utility, and false-negative characterization in some studies. | Need richer clinically framed evaluation, calibration and uncertainty with clinically meaningful thresholds. | Report full confusion matrix, sensitivity/specificity, PPV/NPV, FNR/FPR, balanced accuracy, AUROC/PR-AUC, Brier score, calibration, bootstrap CIs; decision curves only once outcome and care decision are specified. |

## Priority gaps (evidence-supported)

1. **Outcome harmonization and target validity:** culture positivity versus clinical UTI is repeatedly mixed in framing; this is a methodological issue, not a model novelty claim.
2. **Patient-/site-level generalization:** repeated records and single-center/single-network datasets create leakage and transport risks.
3. **Operating point and false negatives:** 2026 sample-level CatBoost has AUROC 0.858 but sensitivity 0.587; 2025 threshold tuning shows marked sensitivity changes. Strong AUC is not enough for a rule-out use case.
4. **Calibration/utility and subgroup behavior:** assess predicted risk reliability, not just ranking, and evaluate age/sex/site only where supported by adequate samples.
5. **Reproducibility/data access:** large studies often do not release individual-level data, preventing independent reproduction. Open supplementary cohorts help but require source-data provenance and patient grouping.

All are provisional until the project datasets are locally audited and the full evidence review is completed. None constitutes a claimed original contribution.
